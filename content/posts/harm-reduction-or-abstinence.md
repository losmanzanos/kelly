A lot of people put off calling a counselor because they think there's a price of admission: you have to already want to quit.

That belief keeps people out of the room for years. It's also not how I work.

## The two approaches, briefly

**Abstinence-based** work takes stopping entirely as the goal. It's the model behind most twelve-step programs and most residential treatment, and for many people it's exactly right — particularly where use has become dangerous, or where moderation has been tried repeatedly and hasn't held.

**Harm reduction** starts from where you are and works to reduce the damage. That might mean using less, using more safely, not using alone, separating use from driving, protecting sleep, or keeping naloxone in the house. Abstinence stays available as a goal; it just isn't a prerequisite for getting help.

## Why the choice isn't a gate

Here's what I've seen: people who come in for harm reduction frequently move toward abstinence on their own, once they're not defending a position. When nobody's demanding a commitment, there's room to look honestly at what use is costing.

The reverse also happens. People who came in certain they wanted abstinence sometimes discover the real problem was the anxiety underneath, and that treating it changed the picture entirely.

You don't have to know which one you are on the first call.

## What we'd actually do

We start with an honest inventory — what you use, how much, when, and what it does for you. That last part matters. Substances usually work, at least at first, and pretending otherwise makes the conversation useless.

Then we look at cost. Sleep, relationships, money, work, health, self-respect. Not to build a case against you, but because most people haven't added it up in one place.

From there we set a goal you actually believe in. A goal you've agreed to in order to end an uncomfortable conversation isn't a goal.

## When abstinence is the clearer call

I'll be direct when I think it is: withdrawal that's medically dangerous, use during pregnancy, overdose history, or a pattern where moderation attempts have consistently failed. Being non-judgmental doesn't mean being non-opinionated. It means the opinion arrives as information, not as a condition of continuing.

## Starting

If you've been putting off a call because you weren't ready to promise anything — you don't have to promise anything. Reach out and we'll figure out what you're actually aiming at.
